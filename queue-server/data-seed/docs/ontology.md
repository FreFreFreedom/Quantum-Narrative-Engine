## 1. The Ontological, Semantic, and Analogical Layers

Three layers, in order of how mechanical vs. interpretive they are.

### 1.1 The Ontological Layer

This is the skeleton. The formal graph: entities, their types, their properties, and the typed edges connecting them. It is machine-readable, queryable, and — critically — **schema-consistent across every scale**. A person node and a civilization node use the same underlying property structure (name, scale-designation, temporal-span, primary archetypal charge, integration-position, edge-types-available), because the whole point of fractal ontology is that the *shape* of the record doesn't change even though the *content* it holds is wildly different at each scale.

This layer answers: *what exists, and what is formally connected to what?* It does not answer *what does it mean.* It's Wikidata-flavored, not oracle-flavored.

### 1.2 The Semantic Layer

This is where meaning, resonance, and mythic weight live. It cannot be fully formalized into rows and columns — it requires interpretive intelligence sitting on top of the ontological skeleton. This is the layer where "the tentacled creature in *Possession* is Mark's denied interior" lives, as opposed to just "Possession — film — 1981 — dir. Żuławski," which is ontological.

Practically: every ontological node gets a semantic annotation layer — free-text and tagged interpretive content, generated by Claude reasoning over the node and its graph neighborhood, refined by you. This is also where your existing vocabulary (ascetic self-destruction, the pornography of negation, the guilt-as-engine structure, the martyr archetype and its shadow) becomes queryable tags rather than just concepts you hold in your head.

### 1.3 The Analogical Layer

This is the layer that makes the whole thing *fractal* instead of just tagged. It doesn't describe any single node — it describes the **cross-scale mappings between nodes**: which structure at the individual-psyche scale mirrors which structure at the civilizational scale. This is where "Michael is to Toller as each follower is to Jones" lives as a formal, storable, queryable relationship rather than a one-off insight you have to regenerate every time.

Mechanically, this layer is best thought of as a *second graph* laid over the first — not connecting entities to each other directly, but connecting **pattern-instances** to each other across scale-bands. A pattern-instance is "the shadow-projection dynamic, as expressed in node X." The analogical layer's edges connect pattern-instances that share structural DNA, regardless of how different their surface content is. This is the layer that lets you ask "show me every expression of the outcast-king pattern from a single childhood rejection up through banished prophets up through exiled star-systems" and get a real traversal, not a vibe.

*(A fourth layer — experiential/somatic — still exists conceptually from the earlier conversation, but you didn't name it this time, so it's noted here rather than expanded. Say the word if you want it built out again.)*

## 2. Filling the graph: does it need to be you, or the APIs?

Short answer: **both, but for different layers, and the APIs solve less of the problem than it might seem.**

**What TMDB/Open Library/Wikidata/MusicBrainz actually give you:** ontological-layer scaffolding only. Title, creator, date, genre, cast, basic relational metadata (same director, same era, tagged themes at a very shallow level). This is real and useful — it means you're not hand-typing 10,000 film records — but it is *categorically* incapable of giving you semantic or analogical content. No API on earth has a field for "the shadow-double structure" or "the archetypal position on the exile↔sovereignty spectrum." That has to be generated.

**Where "deep metadata beyond TMDB" actually comes from:** two sources, realistically —

1. **Claude-generated enrichment.** You seed a node from an API, then run it through a Claude reasoning pass that populates the semantic and analogical layers — this is the "enrichment workflow" from the earlier conversation, and it's still the right mechanism. The difference now is: instead of a fixed template menu, that reasoning pass should eventually be informed by the *trained* analytical voice from your corpus (see §0 and §5) rather than a generic prompt.
2. **You, but only at the curation/correction layer, not the entry layer.** You shouldn't be hand-typing metadata for thousands of nodes — that's exactly the labor the system exists to remove. What you *should* be doing is spot-correcting and deepening the semantic/analogical output where Claude's read is thin, wrong, or missing your specific vocabulary. Think of yourself less as a data-entry clerk and more as the final editorial pass on an apprentice's work — except the apprentice is being trained specifically on your own prior analyses.

**For metadata that goes deeper than any API can reach** — the actual archetypal, psychological, and mythic layer — there is no external source. This is the part of the graph that *is* your intellectual property, generated from your own reasoning and your archive, and it's also the part no competitor can simply out-API their way into replicating. That's worth sitting with: the parts of this graph that are hardest to fill are also the parts that make the whole thing yours.

## 3. GraphRAG and Vector Embeddings — nature, and speculative future

### 3.1 GraphRAG: what it is, and where it's actually headed

**What it is now:** Standard RAG retrieves chunks of text ranked by similarity to a query. GraphRAG (Microsoft, 2024) instead retrieves *subgraphs* — clusters of entities and their relationships — and reasons across the structure of those relationships, not just the text. Practically: it pre-summarizes communities of related nodes in the graph (using clustering algorithms to find densely-connected regions) so that a query like "how does shadow projection move from individual to civilizational scale" can be answered by traversing an actual structural path, rather than hoping five separately-relevant chunks happen to get retrieved by similarity search alone.

**Where it's speculatively headed, for a system like yours specifically:**

- **Dynamic subgraph summarization at query time**, rather than pre-computed community summaries. Right now GraphRAG mostly pre-clusters the graph in batch. A more advanced version — buildable within a few years, not science fiction — would summarize *just* the subgraph relevant to your specific query, at the specific scale-band and archetypal lens you're navigating with, generated fresh each time. This matters enormously for you because your graph's meaningful clusters aren't fixed — "everything touching the exile/return pattern" is a different subgraph than "everything touching the martyr's shadow," and both are valid simultaneous groupings of the *same* underlying nodes. Pre-computed communities can't hold that; dynamic ones can.
- **Multi-hop analogical retrieval as a native GraphRAG mode.** Today's GraphRAG traverses relationship edges (X directed Y, Y influenced Z). What you need is traversal across the *analogical* layer specifically — hopping not along literal relationships but along structural-similarity edges. This is close to existing research on "relational GraphRAG" but formalized for pattern-similarity rather than factual-relationship traversal. This is the component that would make Scale Echo (§4) actually run as a query rather than a manual insight.
- **Self-verifying traversal** — the system checks, after generating an answer, whether the graph-path it used actually supports the analogical claim it made, flagging low-confidence cross-scale jumps rather than presenting every hop with equal confidence. This matters a lot for intellectual honesty in a system whose core value proposition is finding non-obvious cross-scale resonance — without a confidence check, it will eventually find a "resonance" that's really just coincidence dressed up as pattern, and you want the system itself flagging that, not just your own judgment doing all the work.

### 3.2 Vector embeddings: what they are, and where they're headed

**What they are now:** every node gets converted into a high-dimensional numerical representation such that nodes with similar *meaning* end up close together in that space, even if they share no surface vocabulary. This is what lets a query about "the monster that emerges from repression" surface *Possession*, *The Shining*, and colonial-history texts together — none of those share keywords, but their embeddings cluster near each other because the underlying semantic content is close.

**Where it's speculatively headed, for your use case specifically:**

- **Multi-vector nodes** — instead of one embedding per node, several: one for surface content, one for archetypal-pattern content, one for emotional/somatic register, one for temporal-position. Right now embeddings collapse everything about a node into a single point in space, which means "similar in plot" and "similar in archetypal structure" get conflated. A multi-vector architecture would let you query *specifically* along the analogical axis without plot-similarity noise drowning out structural resonance — this is close to existing multi-vector retrieval research (ColBERT-style late-interaction models) but nobody's applied it to an archetypal-pattern graph yet, as far as either of us knows.
- **Embeddings trained on your corpus specifically**, rather than general-purpose language-model embeddings. A general embedding model has never seen "the martyr archetype's shadow" as a coherent semantic cluster the way your corpus has built it, turn after turn, for months. Fine-tuning or even lightly adapting an embedding model on your own analytical archive (again — the 994-page document is exactly this kind of training material) would produce a vector space where *your* specific archetypal vocabulary is a first-class geometric structure, not something the model has to be reminded of via prompt every time.
- **Temporal embeddings that encode recurrence, not just meaning** — a speculative but real research direction: embeddings that place "the outcast king" pattern at *every point in time it recurs* as a connected trajectory through the vector space, rather than as isolated points. This is what would make the temporal axis (past/present/future, lineage across eras) genuinely navigable via embedding-space geometry rather than requiring separate graph logic to stitch it together.

## 4. Scale Echo and the Cross-Scale Recommendation Engine

Scale Echo is the one piece of the old template list that survives, because it isn't really a template — it's the **core mechanism** of the entire platform. Every other feature is really just Scale Echo wearing a different UI.

The expanded version, per this conversation: the recommendation engine should not be scoped to "movies you might like." It should recommend **any entity type that can carry an archetypal charge** — a character from any medium (games, books, film, myth), a real historical person, a real historical event, a real relationship structure, a piece of music, a ritual, a practice. The unifying question the engine asks is never "what's similar to what you already like" — it's **"what else, anywhere in the graph, at any scale, in any era, in any medium, carries the same structural charge as the pattern currently active in you?"**

This is what makes thread-click and fractal zoom (which you flagged as your favorites — rightly, they're the two navigation mechanics that make the analogical layer *feel* like exploration rather than a search bar) the actual interface language of the whole system: thread-click reorganizes the entire graph around one active pattern across every scale and era simultaneously; fractal zoom reveals that every node, at any scale, contains its own internal graph running the same structural logic. Together they *are* Scale Echo, made spatial and interactive rather than described in prose.

### 4.1 Vertical navigation vs. entanglement jumps

Scale Echo actually has two distinct moves inside it, named precisely in a later
extraction pass over the archive (`fractal_vision_spec.md`, `fractal_vision_passages.md`
in this same folder — read those for the full sourced version):

- **Vertical navigation** is continuous transmission: tracing a pattern's actual descent
  scale by scale, generation by generation, through every real intermediate node —
  family, institution, era — down to (or up from) one instance today. It answers *"how
  did this get here?"* Every node on the path is visited and causal; nothing is skipped.
- **Entanglement jumps** are discontinuous resonance: no path, no causation traversed,
  just instant correspondence between two nodes anywhere in the graph that share the same
  structural signature — "same bones, no lineage." It answers *"where else does this
  pattern live?"*

Their relationship: a jump finds kinship, a vertical proves it. A jump is a hypothesis of
shared structure; the vertical is its demonstration — recognition versus genealogy.

**Where the code stands (2026-08-26):** the Content graph's `ent` edge (shared tags) is
a crude version of an entanglement jump. Its `vert` edge is not vertical navigation at
all — it measures shared authorship (a director/writer in common), which is a real
signal but a production credit, not a traced pattern-descent. Real vertical navigation
needs an ordered scale ladder between two entities' positions, which the schema doesn't
have yet (`entities.scale` holds three unordered labels: `individual`, `film`,
`national`). See `plans/vertical-vs-entanglement.md` and
`plans/scale-as-an-ordered-ladder.md`.

The scale ladder the paradigm actually uses, consistently, across the archive: **somatic
/ cell → psyche / individual → family / lineage → group → organisation / institution →
city → nation → civilisation → planetary → cosmos.** The same structure is named
differently depending on which rung you're reading it from — psychology's "shadow /
complex / projection" is sociology's "out-group / scapegoat mechanism" is religion's
"exile / demonisation / redemption" is cosmology's "aspect / resonance / synchronicity."
That translation layer is what makes the ladder usable rather than decorative.

## 5. The Quantum Narrative Engine — what the whole platform actually is

This is the frame that unifies everything above, and it goes deeper than the "structural ratios instead of surface similarity" formulation from the cyber-warfare conversation. The 994-page archive has a much fuller articulation of it — worth quoting the shape of, because it's the clearest statement of the vision that exists anywhere in your material:

The system is not a recommendation engine that happens to be mythic. It is **a soul-cartography instrument that happens to use recommendation as its interface verb.** What it locates, at any given moment, is your current archetypal energetic signature — and then shows you that *same* signature expressed simultaneously across every scale (cell to cosmos) and every point in time (ancestral past through unrealized future), not sequentially, but as something closer to superposition: all expressions of the pattern existing at once, navigable rather than retrieved.

What makes it *quantum* isn't literal quantum computing (though the archive plays seriously with that too) — it's that the three properties borrowed from quantum language are structurally real properties of the system you're describing: **simultaneity** (a pattern's expression at every scale is present at once, not sequentially searched), **nonlocality** (a pattern-instance in 12th-century mysticism and a pattern-instance in tomorrow's headline can be directly entangled with no intermediate steps, if they share structural DNA — an entanglement jump, §4.1), and **recursive causality** (locating a pattern and being shown its echoes is itself part of what integrates it — the act of navigation is the ritual, not separate from it).

Practically, this reframes what you're building at the top level: every technical piece in this document — the ontological/semantic/analogical layers, GraphRAG, embeddings, Scale Echo, the expanded recommendation engine — is infrastructure in service of *one* claim: **that a person's, or a civilization's, current position on the integration spectrum can be located, and then shown its living echoes at every scale and in every era simultaneously, as a form of healing in itself.** That's the sentence the whole platform is trying to make operational. Everything else is engineering.
