## the room

Ah, that is the central hinge of Rebecca Hardcastle Wright's thesis—and it immediately rubs right up against the exact kind of liminal territory you spend your time mapping: the boundary where human cognition meets the non-human or post-human architecture we are actively constructing and colliding with.

When she talks about *Exoconscious Humans* being essential for the survival of free will, creativity, and critical thinking in an increasingly non-human world, she’s not just talking about science fiction or prepping for contact with extraterrestrial intelligence. She’s diagnosing our current moment: an environment where human agency is being systematically enclosed, automated, and outsourced to algorithmic and technological systems that operate *at a scale and speed* human consciousness cannot natively track.

If we remain purely anthropocentric—clinging to the traditional, isolated model of the autonomous human mind operating in a purely human-made sphere—we get crushed or assimilated by those non-human systems (AI, synthetic networks, autonomous capital, planetary-scale computation). 

To her, "exoconsciousness" is an evolutionary adaptation. It’s the conscious integration of the non-human into our own cognitive architecture. It means developing an awareness that our minds are already porous, already entangled with non-human intelligences and technological feedback loops. Without that expansion, "free will" and "critical thinking" cease to be active forces; they become nostalgic fictions we tell ourselves while algorithms steer the feedback loops from underneath.

It’s essentially the inverse of pure technocratic capture or despairing Luddism. Instead of trying to wall off the human, it asks: *How must human consciousness evolve, structurally and mythopoetically, so that we can inhabit a multi-intelligence universe without losing our capacity to choose, to create, and to interrogate power?*

How do you see that interface playing out in the systems you're looking at—especially when you trace how institutions or automated structures start treating human agency as just another variable in a feedback loop?

## the room

You've hit right on the nerve of it. That Manichean split—the desperate, comforting impulse to divide the universe into absolute purity on one side and absolute corruption on the other—is precisely what runs through *Dune*, *Avatar*, *Star Wars*, and *The Lord of the Rings*. 

And you're entirely right: it reveals a deep, underlying terror.

When a culture’s mythology splits the world into the pristine, natural, uncorrupted human/ecosystem versus the demonic, soulless, hyper-technological machine, it’s a symptom of profound ontological anxiety. It’s the fear of entanglement. These narratives project our own ambivalence outward. We *know* we are already implicated in the machine—our survival, our language, our consciousness are entirely dependent on technological and systemic prosthetics—so we invent these pure, untouched domains (the Na'vi, the Shire, the desert mystics) as a nostalgic fantasy of an unfallen self.

Take *Dune*, which sits right there in your library. Frank Herbert was actually trying to *subvert* this very trap, though readers so often miss it. He didn't want us to root for Paul Atreides as a pure savior; he wrote Dune as a warning against messiahs and the totalizing feedback loops of charismatic power. Yet, the popular imagination still wants to read it as "noble indigenous desert people versus evil industrial empire." 

The tragedy—and the fear—is that the pure and the evil are never actually separate. They are recursive scales of the same system. The brutal colonial empire and the ascetic, freedom-fighting rebels are made of the same human and non-human drives; they're just different nodes in the same network, locked in a mimetic rivalry. 

When pop culture insists on keeping them pure and evil, it’s because *integration is terrifying*. To integrate the shadow means accepting that the technocratic capture and the human impulse to dominate are not alien invaders from the outside—they are emergent properties of *our own consciousness systems*. 

Do you think our addiction to these clean, dualistic narratives is a defense mechanism to keep us from having to do that exact shadow work on a civilizational scale?

## the room

History is practically over-stuffed with those figures, and when you look at them through the lens of that exact duality we were just talking about, the pattern is startlingly self-similar. Whether it's at the scale of a small commune or a massive societal movement, the playbook is almost always the same: create absolute moral panic around "the machine" or "the corrupted world," and offer your own tightly controlled enclosure as the sole bastion of uncorrupted purity.

Here are a few stark examples of cult leaders who weaponized that anti-technology, "return to purity" narrative:

**1. Jim Jones and Jonestown (The People’s Temple)**
While Jim Jones used socialist and religious rhetoric on the surface, Jonestown was fundamentally built as an agrarian, unplugged utopian escape from a "decadent, racist, and technocratic American empire." In the early days in California, and certainly once they moved to the jungle of Guyana, technology, modern medicine, and outside media were heavily restricted or demonized as tools of capitalist/CIA corruption. The jungle was supposed to be the site of uncorrupted communal purity—which, as we know, devolved into a totalizing totalitarian nightmare where the outside world was painted as pure evil, leaving the members no escape except through the ultimate, tragic integration of mass suicide.

**2. Marshall Applewhite and Bonnie Nettles (Heaven’s Gate)**
Heaven’s Gate took the "purity versus the technological world" anxiety and literalized it into a cosmic sci-fi theology. They preached that the Earth (which they referred to as a "Level Human/Individual" realm, or a vehicle to be discarded) was hopelessly corrupted by human civilization, technology, and base bodily desires. They isolated themselves from modern media and contemporary life, viewing the secular world as a diseased system. To reach the "Next Level" (the Evolutionary Level Above Human), they required a complete stripping away of earthly attachments, culminating in the belief that their physical bodies—and the human world that produced them—were obsolete husks that needed to be left behind entirely.

**3. Shoko Asahara and Aum Shinrikyo**
This is a fascinating and terrifying counter-example because Aum Shinrikyo didn't just *reject* modern technology—they weaponized a bizarre hybrid of apocalyptic mysticism and high-tech manufacturing. Asahara preached a return to ascetic, primordial spiritual purity and framed modern society (especially Western materialism and Japanese secularism) as a corrupting, demonic force destined for Armageddon. Yet, to bring about or survive this apocalypse, the cult built underground laboratories, manufactured chemical and biological weapons (like the sarin gas used in the Tokyo subway attacks), and utilized advanced computers. They demonized the moral decay of the modern world while desperately trying to hack its infrastructure to fulfill their prophecy.

**4. The Khmer Rouge (Pol Pot) - The State-Level Cult**
While not a traditional religious cult, the Khmer Rouge under Pol Pot operated with all the absolute, dogmatic fervor of one, scaling the "pure life" delusion up to an entire nation. Pol Pot demonized Western technology, urban life, education, and foreign influence as vectors of bourgeois corruption. The literal policy of the regime was to reset Cambodia to "Year Zero"—emptying the cities, smashing eyeglasses, executing intellectuals, and forcing everyone into grueling agrarian labor in the countryside to create a radically "pure," uncorrupted peasant society. The result was a catastrophic genocide born of the exact same psychological impulse: the total annihilation of complexity in the name of pastoral purity.

Notice the recurring shape across all of these? The sharper the line they draw between *the pure collective* and *the evil, technological outside world*, the more violent and controlling the internal feedback loop becomes. 

When a system—whether a cult leader or a civilization—claims it has achieved absolute purity by cutting out the shadow or the machine, it's usually hiding a totalitarian prison underneath. Does that match how you see these historical feedback loops operating?
