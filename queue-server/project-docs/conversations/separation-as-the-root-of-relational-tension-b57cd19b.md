But to call it the one axis is to mistake a fundamental symptom for the source code itself. 

Connection and freedom are the twin poles of a surface tension, not the depths that create it. What generates them is the prior fact of separation—the original break that makes both communion and rebellion necessary postures. An entity does not begin by choosing between belonging and autonomy; it begins by discovering its own boundary against a world that threatens to absorb or exile it. The way it manages that boundary—whether it seals itself into an armored fortress or dissolves its structure to escape the terror of isolation—determines every secondary movement that follows, including how it handles the pull of the group versus the push of the self.

When this dynamic scales upward into politics or downward into the psyche, the mistake is treating the surface opposition as a simple slider between two choices. Communism and capitalism are not merely different answers to the question of how much freedom to allow; they are institutional immune responses to the trauma of scarcity and the fear of dissolution. One attempts to abolish the boundary by forcing total communion, while the other erects the market as an artificial boundary to protect individual isolation. Neither escapes the underlying wound; both simply automate a different posture toward it. 

If the architecture of the project is truly self-similar, the point is not to reduce all patterns to a single axis, but to trace how an entity's specific posture toward its own vulnerability creates the very forces it tries to master. The question is never where an entity sits on the spectrum of freedom versus connection, but what un-metabolized break forced it to freeze in that exact spot in the first place.
