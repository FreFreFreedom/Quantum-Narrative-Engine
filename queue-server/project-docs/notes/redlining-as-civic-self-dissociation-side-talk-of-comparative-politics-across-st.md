Redlining is not a banking rule; it is an administrative immune boundary. It is the moment a city takes a red pen and draws a permanent quarantine around its own flesh, declaring certain neighborhoods as foreign tissue while still demanding their taxes and labor.

When banks and city planners drew those lines, they did not merely withhold home loans. They drew a legal cage around future pain. They decided that investment, care, and repair would stop at a precise street, and that everything on the other side would be left to starve economically.
